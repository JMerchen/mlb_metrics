What if?

Swap a starting QB, mark players out, and set how well each unit plays. Each team's run game, pass game, run defense and pass defense are rated in percent of their normal yards per play (a defense at 90% allows 10% more). A player out lowers the units his position touches by a default - what an absence at that position has cost teams in results, scaled by how much he plays - and you can type over it for any player ("run game at 50% without him") or set a whole unit outright. The yards become points and the points become win chance, the same way Vegas turns point spreads into odds. A QB swap reruns our model with that QB's rating; "Average starter" and "an average regular" cover anyone not listed. Players follow their current roster. Team units

How strong each team's units are, on a scale where 100 is league average and higher is always stronger (like OPS+ or ERA+). Every 30 points is about one standard deviation: 130 is top-six territory, 160 is elite, 70 and 40 the same distance below. Each number is points added (or allowed) per play. By default recent games count more (reaching back into last season) and each unit is adjusted for the strength of the opponents it has faced - the combination that best predicted the next game over 2017-2026; switch either off to see this season's games counted equally, or unadjusted. QB+ is the QB listed to start this week, so a backup starting shows up here. Tap a column to sort.

Payroll value

Winning isn't the only measure of success - winning for the money is. Each team's season is measured against its own league year. Spending is summed from OverTheCap's player contracts, 2014 on: cash paid (what teams actually paid, signing bonuses counted when paid - it varies most) or cap spending (the cap charges; the hard cap keeps everyone close). Dead money - players since released - isn't included. Effectiveness above 1 means a team bought more wins per dollar than the league did; expected wins are what the seasons that spent most like it won (any earlier season, from 2015).
