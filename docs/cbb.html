Play it out

Picking the favorite in every game is the single likeliest bracket, but no tournament goes that way: past ones have had about 20 upsets of our favorite in 67 games (as few as 13, as many as 26). A simulated bracket plays every game as a weighted coin flip, so a 20% underdog wins one time in five. That is the same thing the chances above come from, done 20,000 times. Run it a few times to see how different two plausible brackets can be.

Team rankings

Rating is how many points better than an average Division I team a team is over 100 possessions (about a game and a half), after adjusting for who it has played and where. It splits into offense (points scored above average) and defense (points allowed below average), each with its national rank. Early in the season the ratings lean on last season's; by January this season's games carry them. Deep dive adds the same thing as plain numbers - points scored and allowed per 100 possessions against an average team (what other sites call adjusted offensive and defensive efficiency) - and each team's chance of beating an average team on a neutral floor.

How teams from different years are compared

Each team's rating is its end-of-season one, tournament games included, measured against the 50 best teams of its own season. So this compares how far each team stood above the good teams of its day - not how a 2010 roster would handle today's pace, three-point shooting or older, transfer-built lineups, which nothing here can know. (Against the average Division I team instead, the last two seasons would take most of the top spots: the best teams have pulled further away from the pack lately.) Games are on a neutral floor at an average pace, and a single game lands about 11 points either side of the expected margin, the same as in our real picks.
