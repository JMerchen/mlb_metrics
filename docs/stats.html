Wins above replacement

Our own measure of what each player was worth in a season. Every pitch changes how many runs the batting team can expect in that exact situation - the outs, the runners, the count - so a single with the bases loaded is worth more than one with nobody on. Batting is what a hitter added that way, graded for the pitchers and parks he faced. Pitching is the same from the other side. Fielding asks of every ball in play how often a ball hit that hard, at that angle, to that spot becomes an out: a fielder who turns a 30% ball into an out gets most of an out's worth, and one who lets a 90% ball fall loses nearly all of one. A hitter robbed by a great play is not charged for the out, and a pitcher is not credited for it.

Each part is measured against the average at his position - a catcher's bat against catchers', a starter against starters. Above replacement adds the gap between average and the kind of player a team calls up when its roster runs out, and WAR divides by the runs a win takes (9.7). Running is what a runner adds on the bases: steals, and extra bases (or outs) on teammates' hits against what is usual for that play. What it cannot see: where fielders were standing, so range is judged from where the ball went, and a catcher's pitch framing and throwing. Click a heading to sort. Teams: what the players add up to, and what is left

A team made of replacement players would still win some games - about 44 of 162. Team WAR is the wins a team's players add on top of that, so the baseline plus Team WAR is its expected wins: what its hitters, runners, fielders and pitchers were worth, turned into wins. (The players' runs go through the runs-to-wins curve together rather than being added up player by player, because an extra run is worth less to a team already far from average.) The gap to its real record is split in two. Not credited is wins in its real run differential that no player's ledger holds: catchers, throwing arms, wild pitches, and whatever our models miss. Close games is how its runs fell across games - winning more than its runs say. Over our two seasons neither leftover carried strongly from one half of a season to the other (about 0.2, against 0.5 for expected wins), which is what luck looks like, but two seasons are too few to call either one pure luck. Hitters

Three views of each hitter's season. WAVE is his batting average for the season (hits per at-bat; his recent weeks don't count extra, because a test showed they predict nothing more), and hit chance his chance of at least one hit in 3.5 at-bats at that rate. CAVE (Carried Average) is the share of his games with at least one hit - a 1-for-4 and a 4-for-4 count the same. WOPS is his OPS. Those two blend the full season with his last 81, 30 and 10 days, so recent games count more. vs. pitchers faced credits hits off tough pitchers more and soft ones less, based on what every pitcher he faced allowed all season. Decision+ grades a hitter's swing-or-take choices: every pitch he saw is placed by where it crossed his strike zone, the count, outs, runners and pitch type, and scored by what his choice was worth over what the league usually does in that spot (league-average results, so it's the decision, not the contact). 100 is average; every 10 points is one standard deviation.
